# [Waveform Formation]Real-time Underwater 3D Acoustic Imaging with Multibeam Forming Based on Orthogonal Line Arrays [with MATLAB Code]

Introduction: Underwater 3D Imaging as a Promising Ocean Exploration Technology, Its Importance is Increasing. In the past, 3D sonar was mainly used for pipeline monitoring, structural inspection, and channel measurement in high-turbidity conditions. It captures three-dimensional information of underwater targets, which are now utilized in autonomous underwater vehicles (AUV) for obstacle avoidance. However, the requirement for a large number of uniform planar arrays limits the system's real-time performance.
Many sparse array design techniques [1]-[5] have been proposed to overcome this limitation. In sparse array methods, the number of sensor units is optimally reduced and distributed over the same aperture as the uniform planar array to maintain similar image resolution. However, sparse arrays retain relatively high numbers of elements, and real-time 3D beamforming remains challenging. Mills cross array has been used as an alternative to uniform planar arrays for underwater 3D acoustic imaging [6]-[7]. In the Mills-cross array, one linear array processes transmission while another handles reception. It requires multiple signal transmissions and scans, limiting its ability to perform in real time. Besides cross arrays, different combinations of horizontal and vertical linear arrays have also been explored for 2D DOA estimation in radio astronomy and radar [8]-[9]. In radar and radio astronomy, the use of spherical coordinates differs from that used in underwater imaging. Moreover, L-shaped and cross-shaped arrays with product beamforming reception arrays lead to multi-target ambiguities as explained in [8]. To our knowledge, there is very limited literature on using orthogonal line arrays (not cross arrays) for underwater 3D acoustic imaging.
In the field of signal processing, several fast beamforming algorithms have been proposed to reduce the computational complexity of traditional three-dimensional beamforming algorithms. In [10], Murino et al. suggested combining dynamic focusing with Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) beamforming for short-range imaging using narrowband signals. Han et al. introduced a distributed parallel subarray (DPS) beamforming for narrowband transmission in [11]. A similar approach was proposed in [12] where Zhao et al. utilized a strategy to divide the linear receiving array of a cross-array into two subarrays. A similar method was presented in [13] where Zhao et al. exploited a strategy to divide the linear receiving array of a cross-array into two subarrays. Rypkema et al. introduced a method for approximate 3D beamforming applications involving small arrays in [13]. Recently, Zhao et al. introduced the concept of fine grid sparse arrays along with new beamforming algorithms for narrowband underwater imaging systems in [1]. All the above mentioned beamforming algorithms are performed in the frequency domain and are widely used for narrowband transmission. Two beamforming algorithms for broadband transmission were described in [14] and [15]. Chi et al. proposed an effective but approximate broadband transmission three-dimensional sonar imaging method in [14]. Palmese et al. proposed an accurate three-dimensional beamforming method based on chirp zeta transform (CZT) for broadband transmission in [15].
The fast beamforming algorithms and sparse array technologies documented in the literature effectively reduce the computational time required for 3D beamforming and facilitate the generation of 3D images in near real-time. This efficiency sometimes leads to trade-offs in image resolution. However, these algorithms' key application areas such as obstacle avoidance require further reduction in computation time and array complexity. In this work, we utilize a novel 2-D L-shaped linear array with very limited sensor elements combined with nonlinear beamforming to create real-time three-dimensional underwater images. The method is implemented in the time domain, ensuring that the algorithm complexity remains consistent regardless of the bandwidth of the transmitted signal.
The proposed work aims to conduct further research in two broad areas of underwater 3D acoustic imaging, as described below.
1) Efficient Array Design: A uniform planar array is an ideal choice for 3D imaging; however, the cost of design and computation prompts researchers to explore linear array combinations and sparse arrays as alternatives. In this work, we propose using a planar array with the edges of a uniform rectangular array instead of a far-field 3D imaging with a uniform planar array. During reconstruction, only L-shaped configurations are used to reconstruct each sector. This modification not only reduces hardware complexity but also minimizes memory requirements.
2) Nonlinear Multiplication Beamforming: Traditionally, narrowband transmission has been used for underwater imaging. Therefore, most fast beamforming algorithms are proposed in the frequency domain. The main obstacle preventing time-domain beamforming for underwater 3D imaging is the significant computational complexity generated by hardware interpolation filters [16]. In scenarios where wideband transmission is required for high-resolution imaging [17], and software interpolation prevails in multicore computing systems, it is expected that the computational time of direct frequency domain methods will exceed that of time-domain beamforming [18]. This paper presents a novel effective time-domain nonlinear beamforming method for real-time 3D acoustic imaging underwater. The algorithm improves image resolution and minimizes sidelobe impact, resulting in reduced sensor elements.
The article presents a new quadrant-based nonlinear beamforming algorithm, which utilizes an L-shaped array (placed at the edges of a large rectangular array) to achieve acoustic three-dimensional imaging in the time domain. Compared with three state-of-the-art methods, the proposed method demonstrates reduced computational complexity and time, improved corner resolution by 1 μ m, and increased peak sidelobe level by 11.8 dB. Despite the increase in background noise due to sidelobes, the use of this method allows for the background noise to be confined within the quadrant and can be appropriately thresholded. A thorough analysis of the far-field three-dimensional acoustic imaging using an L-shaped array is completed using point spread functions. The method was applied to reconstruct three-dimensional images of two different underwater targets, achieving three-dimensional reconstruction of underwater targets without degrading image quality. The experimental results for both underwater targets also confirm the same observations, reducing simulated computation time by 97 times.
